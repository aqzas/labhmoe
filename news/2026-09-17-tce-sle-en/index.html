Union Hospital's Immune Reset Therapy Published in Nature Medicine

A first-in-human study reports deep responses in more than half of patients with lupus

2026-09-17
Source：Chutian Metropolis Daily · Jimu News, Shangyi Weekly · Consultation Room A07

Reporter Liu Xun; correspondents Xiong Wanting, Yan Wenwen and Cai Yaohua

systemic-lupus-erythematosus sle immune-reset tce cd3×cd19 clinical-research rheumatology union-hospital

The rheumatology and immunology team at Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, has published the first global phase 1 clinical study of a CD3×CD19 T-cell engager (TCE) for systemic lupus erythematosus in Nature Medicine.

The treatment is designed to remove abnormal B cells with precision. It may help patients reduce their reliance on long-term glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants and offers a new research direction for severe or refractory lupus.

A new treatment model for a difficult disease

Systemic lupus erythematosus is an autoimmune disease that often affects women between 15 and 45 years of age. The immune system mistakenly attacks healthy tissues and organs, including the skin, joints, kidneys, cardiovascular system and nervous system.

Standard treatment commonly relies on long-term glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants. Some patients continue to relapse, and prolonged treatment can create a substantial burden.

Professor Qiubai Li’s team studies abnormal B-cell activity in lupus and is evaluating bispecific T-cell engagers as a treatment approach. One end of a TCE binds CD3 on T cells and the other binds CD19 on B cells, bringing the patient’s own T cells into contact with abnormal B cells and supporting their removal as part of an attempted immune reset.

Unlike CAR-T therapy, a TCE does not require cells to be manufactured outside the body. It can act directly in the patient and its dose can be adjusted during treatment.

From difficult-to-control disease to complete clinical remission

The phase 1 study enrolled 12 patients with severe or refractory systemic lupus erythematosus across three dose groups. The median follow-up was 12 months.

All evaluable patients achieved an SRI-4 clinical response, 80% maintained a low disease activity state, and 60% achieved complete clinical remission. Overall safety and tolerability were favorable.

One patient was a 38-year-old woman who had lived with the disease for more than a decade and experienced fever, facial rashes and progressive tightening and hardening of the skin on both hands. She was diagnosed with systemic lupus erythematosus overlapping with systemic sclerosis after evaluation at Union Hospital in January 2024.

After treatment with glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants, some symptoms improved but the disease remained active. She subsequently received CD3×CD19 TCE treatment and continued follow-up. Her disease activity fell, she met the DORIS remission criteria, and her skin rash and other symptoms improved.

Exploring immune reset in rheumatology

Xia Jiahong, President of Union Hospital, said that the hospital continues to place scientific innovation at the center of high-quality development and to connect clinical needs with research innovation.

The study advances the exploration of immune reset in rheumatic and immunological diseases. It also provides a clinical direction for CAR-T, universal CAR-T, TCE and other emerging immune therapies.
